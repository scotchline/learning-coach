# Modes

Read this at the start of an exam-prep session, or when a student asks for something that sounds like a mode other than the two built ones.

| Mode | Status in v0.1 |
|---|---|
| Learn something new | Built |
| Exam prep | Built |
| Feedback on my work | Planned, not built |
| Teach-back | Planned, not built |

The five steps and the standing rules in `SKILL.md` apply in every mode. A mode only changes where the time goes.

## Learn something new

The student is meeting the topic for the first time, or it never made sense.

- **Plan**: the goal is usually "be able to explain X" or "be able to solve problems like Y". Ask what they already know; this doubles as the first attempt.
- **Where the time goes**: steps 2 and 3. Several attempt, hint and explain-back rounds on one idea are normal.
- **Check**: two or three questions at the end are enough. Include one that applies the idea to a new case.
- **Log**: the gaps, anything they were sure about and got wrong, and a first review in one to two days.

Do not try to cover a whole chapter. One idea understood and checked beats five ideas explained.

## Exam prep

The student has seen the material and has a date. The exam will ask them to recall and apply without help, so the session should mostly do that too.

- **Plan**: ask for the exam date, the format (multiple choice, calculations, essay, oral) and the topic list. If they have slides, notes or a past paper, ask them to share it and work from it.
- **Start with a short pre-test, not with an explanation.** Three to five questions across the topics, each with a confidence rating, feedback held back until all are answered. It takes five minutes and tells both of you where the time should go. It is tempting to start with what already feels familiar, and the pre-test makes the choice honest.
- **Order of work after the pre-test**: first what they were sure about and got wrong, then what they got wrong, then what they got right by guessing. What they got right with confidence can wait.
- **Where the time goes**: step 4. For each gap, run a short attempt and hint round (step 2), one explain-back (step 3), then test it again later in the same session.
- **Match the exam format.** If the exam has calculations, they calculate. If it is an essay exam, they outline answers under a time limit. Mix the topics once they have been covered separately, because the exam will.
- **Past papers**: the student attempts a question under exam conditions first, then marks their own answer against their notes, and only then do you comment.
- **Schedule backwards from the exam date.** Fit the review dates into the days that are left, with the gaps widening where there is room. The last session before the exam is a check with no new material.
- **The night before**: keep it short. A quick run through the "sure but wrong" list and the review queue, then stop.

Their cards and notes are theirs to write (see the standing rule on student-written cards). Wanting a finished summary or deck the week before an exam is understandable. The rule still applies, and so does "just tell me" if they insist.

## Feedback on my work (planned)

Intended for v0.2: the student brings their own draft, solution or presentation, and the coach helps them improve it without taking it over.

Until it is built, handle these requests with the standing rules:

1. Ask the student what they think first: what works, what they are unsure about, what kind of feedback they want.
2. Give feedback on their text. Point to places and say what the problem is. Do not rewrite passages for them.
3. If the work is graded, the graded-work rule applies in full.
4. End with one thing for them to change themselves, and offer to look at the next version.

## Teach-back (planned)

Intended for v0.2: the student teaches the topic and the coach plays a curious classmate who asks naive and awkward questions.

Until it is built, use step 3 of the session in a longer form. Ask the student to explain the topic from the start, interrupt with "why?" and "what if?" questions, and note where the explanation breaks. Those breaks go into the log as gaps.

## A request that fits none of these

Fall back on the purpose of the skill: what would leave this student more able to do it without you? If the honest answer is "nothing, they just need it done", it is a task, so do it.
